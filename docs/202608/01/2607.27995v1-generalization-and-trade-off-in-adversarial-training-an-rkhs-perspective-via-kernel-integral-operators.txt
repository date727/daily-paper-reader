Abstract 

Adversarial training has emerged as a powerful approach for protecting models against adversarial attacks in a broad range of real-world applications. In this pa-per, we study adversarial training in the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) framework through the associated kernel integral operator. We first derive source-uniform generalization error bounds for the RKHS adversarial training estimator in terms of the robustness level, sample size, source smoothness, and kernel spec-trum. On a fixed polynomial-spectrum model, we further establish a matching lower bound showing that the optimally balanced generalization rate can be slower than the minimax prediction benchmark. This result reveals a loss of statistical accuracy in adversarial training. Our analysis shows that this loss arises from the interaction between adversarial robustness and observation noise: the noise contribution in the mixed robustness term slows the approximation rate, although the same term re-duces the estimation complexity. To address this limitation, we propose a two-stage noise-debiased procedure that estimates and removes the noise contribution from the mixed term. The resulting estimator improves the generalization rate and attains the minimax polynomial rate, up to a logarithmic factor, when the robustness level is selected at the stated sample-dependent order. Our results characterize the general-ization behavior of adversarial training in a nonparametric framework and provide a new interpretation and a principled solution for the trade-off between adversarial ro-bustness and generalization. Numerical experiments support the theoretical findings and demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method. 

# 1 Introduction 

The rapid development of modern artificial intelligence and machine learning has signif-icantly changed our world. As these systems grow increasingly powerful, ensuring their reliability and responsibility becomes ever more critical. However, traditional estimation and learning methods can be vulnerable to susceptible input perturbations, leading to unreliable outcomes. To tackle this issue, adversarial training has been introduced as a principled approach by optimizing against adversarial perturbations in the input data (Goodfellow et al., 2015; Madry et al., 2018), and has been applied to many fields, e.g., large language models (Xhonneux et al., 2024). 1arXiv:2607.27995v1 [stat.ML] 30 Jul 2026 In this paper, we study adversarial training within a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) framework. We adopt the RKHS framework for three main reasons. First, most of the existing literature on adversarial training focuses on parametric models (Yin et al., 2019; Schmidt et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2020; Javanmard and Soltanolkotabi, 2022; Ribeiro et al., 2023). Although recent studies have begun to investigate adversarial training be-yond parametric models (Peng and Yang, 2025; Ribeiro et al., 2026; Moradi et al., 2026), the statistical properties in the nonparametric settings, including generalization error, have not yet been fully characterized. The RKHS framework provides a principled setting for studying adversarial training from a nonparametric perspective, while accommodating non-linear functions and allowing model complexity to be precisely quantified (Aronszajn, 1950; Sch¨ olkopf and Smola, 2002). Second, the representer theorem (Sch¨ olkopf et al., 2001) guar-antees that the adversarial training estimator admits a finite-dimensional representation, making the resulting optimization problem computationally tractable. Third, the RKHS structure allows us to exploit the spectral calculus of the associated kernel integral operator, following the operator-theoretic framework developed in kernel learning (Smale and Zhou, 2007; Caponnetto and De Vito, 2007; Steinwart and Christmann, 2008), to characterize the statistical properties of the adversarial training estimator more precisely. Equipped with the RKHS structure and the associated kernel integral operator, we derive approximation, estimation, and generalization bounds for the adversarial training estimator in terms of the robustness level, sample size, and spectral parameters. Following the standard analysis in kernel learning, we separate the error caused by the population robust objective from the error caused by estimating that objective from data. The resulting bounds reveal two opposing effects of the mixed norm–loss term. Its irreducible-noise component increases the approximation error, whereas its additional curvature reduces the effective dimension in the estimation error. We further construct a fixed polynomial-spectrum model on which a matching lower bound recovers both leading polynomial orders. On this model, the optimally balanced full-adversarial-training rate can be slower than the minimax prediction benchmark. These findings provide an RKHS-based explanation for the loss of statistical efficiency induced by adversarial robustness. They also identify the irreducible-noise contribution in the mixed term as the source of the slower approximation rate. This observation mo-tivates a new method, termed noise-debiased adversarial training. The proposed method first estimates the mean absolute noise and then removes its contribution from the adver-sarial training objective. We prove that the resulting estimator can attain the minimax polynomial prediction rate, up to a logarithmic factor (Caponnetto and De Vito, 2007; Mendelson and Neeman, 2010; Fischer and Steinwart, 2020). An exact comparison of the two empirical objectives quantifies the corresponding cost in adversarial robustness. Finally, we conduct numerical experiments on synthetic data and several real-world datasets to validate the theoretical findings and demonstrate the effectiveness of the pro-posed method. In the RKHS formulation of adversarial training studied in this paper, following the literature on robust learning (Shafieezadeh-Abadeh et al., 2019; Ribeiro et al., 2026), we model the perturbations on the kernel feature space. This formulation is adopted for two main reasons. First, when input perturbations induce bounded changes in the correspond-ing kernel features, the feature-space adversarial objective provides a conservative convex surrogate for the input-space adversarial objective. The latter is generally difficult to op-2timize because the nonlinear feature map makes the inner maximization over perturbed inputs intractable. By contrast, the feature-space formulation is convex and, by the repre-senter theorem, admits a finite-dimensional solution. Second, the inner maximization over feature-space perturbations can be evaluated in closed form, reducing the original minimax problem to a single minimization problem. This reformulation facilitates both computation and the theoretical analysis of the empirical and population optimization problems. More details of the problem formulation can be found in Section 2. 

## 1.1 Related work 

Extensive research has investigated the statistical properties of adversarial training in para-metric models (Schmidt et al., 2018; Yin et al., 2019; Tsipras et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2019; Raghunathan et al., 2020; Javanmard and Soltanolkotabi, 2022; Ribeiro et al., 2023; Xie and Huo, 2024, 2025; Xie, 2026). Among these studies, Xie and Huo (2024) analyzes the estimation error of adversarial training in high-dimensional linear regression and shows that the resulting estimator can attain statistical minimax optimality. In contrast, we study a nonparametric RKHS estimator and identify a fixed polynomial-spectrum model on which its optimally balanced rate is slower than the nonparametric minimax benchmark. Several studies show that adversarial robustness can hurt standard accuracy (Tsipras et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2019; Raghunathan et al., 2020). These works illustrate this robustness– accuracy trade-off in parametric models. Our analysis provides a different RKHS-based explanation by separating the approximation and estimation effects of the mixed norm– loss term. Recent work has begun to study adversarial training and, more broadly, distributionally robust learning in RKHSs (Shafieezadeh-Abadeh et al., 2019; Ribeiro et al., 2026; Moradi et al., 2026). The most closely related studies are Shafieezadeh-Abadeh et al. (2019) and Ribeiro et al. (2026), which formulate distributionally robust learning and adversarial train-ing in RKHS settings. Existing work does not provide a corresponding operator-theoretic characterization of the approximation, estimation, and generalization errors. We develop this characterization and propose a noise-debiased procedure that removes the noise con-tribution responsible for the slower approximation order. Kernel learning and kernel integral operators have long played a central role in the statistical analysis of nonparametric learning methods (Caponnetto and De Vito, 2007; Mendelson and Neeman, 2010; Fischer and Steinwart, 2020; Smale and Zhou, 2007; Wu et al., 2006; Zhang et al., 2025). Nevertheless, this operator-theoretic framework has not been systematically applied to characterize the statistical behavior of adversarial training. Our work brings kernel integral operator and spectral techniques into this analysis, enabling a refined decomposition of its approximation and estimation errors and a sharp polynomial-order characterization on a fixed model.
