A B S T R A C T 

Despite the success of convolutional neural networks in image classification tasks and their gen-eral application in multi-modal models, their susceptibility to out-of-distribution and adversarial attack samples raises concerns regarding trustworthiness and safety. Among the approaches to tackle such issues, detection methods that analyze the model’s intermediate activations to esti-mate a confidence score are a promising family that evaluates the decision process, relying on a dimensionality reduction step to enable efficient downstream processing of the high-dimensional activations. However, when considering convolutional layers, the dimensionality reduction methods in the literature either lack a mechanism to control the compression/information-loss trade-off or yield large representations. In this paper, we carefully analyze two state-of-the-art detection methods and their dimensionality reductions for convolutional layers and develop a novel reduction method with a controllable high-compression level. We extend these two state-of-the-art detection methods, enabling the usage of any dimensionality reduction, and evaluate their performance on out-of-distribution and adversarial attack detection. Results show that the detection methods with the proposed dimensionality reduction consistently perform better than, or comparable to, the strongest alternative. Furthermore, the proposed method is shown to reduce computation and memory footprints, given that it has the highest compression among the compared methods. 

## 1. Introduction 

The widespread application of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) today raises concerns about their safety and trustworthiness, problems that have recently been formalized in the Artificial Intelligence (AI) Act Cancela-Outeda (2024). Among such models, well-known Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are often used as image classifiers, or as part of multimodal models, e.g., Radford et al. (2021); Song et al. (2018); Segura-Bedmar and Alonso-Bartolome (2022), since they outperform transformer-based models, despite their susceptibility to Out-of-Distribution (OoD) and Adversarial Attack (AA) samples that can easily mislead their predictions. While methods for enhancing the robustness of DNNs have been proposed based on image manipulation Xu et al. (2018), or the model’s outputs Dadalto et al. (2024), solutions that analyze the model’s Intermediate Activations (IAs) estimate a confidence score based on the typicality of its decision process. Among the latter, Deep Mahalanobis Detector (DMD) Lee et al. (2018) and Multi-layer Analysis for Confidence Scoring (MACS) Capelli et al. (2025) analyze the Low-Level Features (LLFs) within the model’s IAs, matching them to layer-wise human-understandable High-Level Features (HLFs), and provide a measurable metric about the model’s internal decision process that is used for OoD and AA detection by analyzing multiple layers. When considering CNNs, the sheer size of the model’s IAs Pintelas et al. (2023) presents a major limitation for detection methods, especially when considering the ever-larger modern models, a problem countered by applying Dimensionality Reduction (DR) methods, e.g., average-based, Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), or Independent Component Analysis (ICA) Chen et al. (2018), which implies the necessity of balancing the compression level and information loss for efficient processing without hindering the downstream analysis.

## 2. Related Works 

The myriad of OoD and AA detection methods for image classifiers can be divided into two macro families: “ante-hoc” methods, which are DNNs with embedded detection mechanisms, requiring specific training and hence not applicable to pre-existing models Liu et al. (2020); Fort et al. (2021); and “post-hoc” methods, which assume a pre-trained DNN. Among the latter, three main sub-families arise. The first family consists of methods that manipulate the DNNs’s inputs, being completely agnostic to the model itself, and generally work by creating sample variations through filtering Xu et al. (2018); Liang et al. (2021) or perturbations Jha et al. (2019) and evaluating the model’s predictions on them. However, such methods are often limited in scope, e.g., to image classifiers, and are vulnerable to AAs devised to avoid detection He et al. (2017). The second family consists of approaches that focus on the model’s outputs. Expanding on the fact that CNNs’ outputs are poorly calibrated Minderer et al. (2021), these approaches create lightweight calibration functions Guo et al. (2017); Perez-Lebel et al. (2023) evaluating only the model’s outputs. Modern approaches in this family create approximations of Monte Carlo dropout models Gal and Ghahramani (2016) using statistical tests Roth et al. (2019) or epistemic uncertainty estimation Dadalto et al. (2024); Yoon and Kim (2024) using samples from a reference dataset, overcoming the distribution shift problems in calibration methods Singh et al. (2021); Wang et al. (2021); Balanya et al. (2024). The third family consists of methods that evaluate the DNNs’ IAs. As examples, Harder et al. (2021) uses Fourier transforms on the activations and a logistic regressor trained on nominal and AA samples, and Gorbett and Blanchard (2022) proposes a Support Vector Machine (SVM) on the penultimate layer’s activations to identify misclassifications, OoD, and AA samples. However, such methods lack a DR step, limiting their scalability to larger models and motivating Chen et al. (2018) to use an ICA-based DR and clustering to identify back-door AAs based on the model’s last hidden layers. As such, methods seldom perform analysis based on the IAs of earlier layers, especially for convolutional models, whose CLs have extremely high dimensionality, thereby requiring non-scalable deep learning methods such as the K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)-based Papernot and McDaniel (2018) and the Coverage Analysis Method (CAM)-based Rossolini et al. (2023) approaches to produce a confidence score by comparing the activations of an inference sample w.r.t. the ones from a reference set. Works that evaluate DNNs’ IAs and leverage DR methods are the focus of this paper. DMD Lee et al. (2018) focuses specifically on CLs, applying a channel-wise high-compression DR to their activations, and estimating the prediction agreement throughout multiple layers with a logistic regressor trained on OoD/AA samples. Extensions of DMD have been proposed by extending its feature association step with layer- and channel-wise Kernel Density Estimations (KDEs) to obtain class-agnostic representations that are classified with a logistic regressor Erdil et al. (2021); and by enriching its dimensionality-reduced representation with a compression-based encoding Yu et al. (2020) 2. MACS Capelli et al. (2025) uses an SVD-based compression, overcoming DMD’s restriction to CLs, and an unsupervised clustering with an empirical posterior analysis to characterize the model’s decision process throughout its layers over nominal samples, overcoming the need for OoD/AA samples for DMD’s regressor fitting. Notably, reducing the dimensionality of CLs is a practical approach, since their high-dimensional features are known for encoding low-dimensional LLFs within their manifold Recanatesi et al. (2019); Ansuini et al. (2019). Such

## 3. Reference Methods 

3.1. Preliminaries 

We target CNN image classifiers ( Φ), whose inputs are multi-channel images ( 𝝃 ) associated with labels ( 𝑙 ) and predictions ̂ 𝑙 = Φ( 𝝃 ) belonging to 𝐶 classes. We consider datasets organized into training ( train ), validation ( val ), and test ( test ) splits 3.We consider OoD and AA IAs-based detection frameworks that fit into the three-stage pipeline illustrated in Figure 1. The first step consists of a DR to obtain a compact version of the activations, referred to as “corevectors” ( 𝝂 )hereinafter, which capture the LLFs present within the IAs to enable efficient downstream processing. In the second step, “feature association”, the corevectors are associated with HLFs 4, resulting in tensors that encode the features’ presence at each layer, referred to as “peepholes” ( 𝝆 ) hereinafter. The third step, “scoring”, estimates a “confidence score” ( 𝑠 ) based on the peepholes from a set of selected layers, estimating whether the sample is In-Distribution (ID)/nominal or OoD/AA. Note that approaches in the literature may implement all three steps, e.g., DMD Lee et al. (2018) and MACS Capelli et al. (2025), or skip some steps, e.g., Rossolini et al. (2023); Harder et al. (2021); Gorbett and Blanchard (2022) do not leverage a DR step. Furthermore, it is common practice to attach the 𝝂 ’s and 𝝆 ’s computation to a subset of 𝐿 layers to limit the computational overhead. Nevertheless, the DR step becomes extremely important since the high-dimensional raw IAs hinder most analysis in real scenarios, especially when the ever-increasing size of modern DNNs is taken into consideration. Therefore, DR methods must also ensure that important LLFs present within the IAs are captured in their result. Special attention must be given to CLs, whose activations span higher dimensions than their linear counterparts, making it difficult for DR methods to effectively compress the activations without significant information loss. Hence, DR methods should also provide control over the trade-off between compression and information loss, enabling the user to tune the detection method. Our goal is to create a DR method for CLs and evaluate its effect on DMD and MACS; thus, we consider the detection methods to be composed of the feature association and scoring steps only, regardless of which compression method is used. Particularly, we leverage lessons from DMD’s and MACS’s original DRs to achieve a controllable high-compression rate.

3.4. MACS 

MACS Capelli et al. (2025) is a general framework that uses an SVD-projection DR to provide controllable compression, and is applicable to layers that can be written as an affine transformation, such as linear ones, or CLs after their kernels are unrolled with a Toeplitz operator Praggastis et al. (2022). The feature association step is composed of a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) clustering and a LLF-to-HLF association based on han Liu and Arik (2020), creating vectors that estimate the presence of HLF in each layer, similar to DMD. And finally, the score compares the information from all layers using empirical estimates.

## 4. Proposed Method 

4.1. Overview 

Figure 2 presents an overview of the proposed method, which takes inspiration from toeplitz and avgpooling to achieve a highly compact representation of CLs’s activations while allowing control over the compression/information-loss trade-off. The main idea is to unroll the CL’s kernels using the dense and compact convolution isomorphism explored in Praggastis et al. (2022), instead of the Toeplitz one used by MACS. From this unrolling, we apply the SVD to create new kernels intrinsically ordered by importance ( ̂ 𝒌 ) and to enable control over the new number of output channels according to the SVD’s trimming parameter ( 𝜅 ). Finally, after applying the convolution to 𝒙 with this new kernel, we leverage avgpooling , yielding a vector with the same dimension as 𝜅 . Therefore, the proposed method, referred to as kernel hereinafter, yields a higher compression level than avgpooling , with compression-level control through 𝜅 , while guaranteeing minimal information loss.

4.3. Implications for OoD and AA Methods 

Considering that we extend MACS and DMD to accept different DR methods, some considerations must be made. Regarding DMD, the corevectors used for generating image variations through back-propagation should not be hindered by the DR’s compression level, and the amount of information retained after compression should be the major factor in its performance; hence the method is expected to yield good performance even when using toeplitz , whose typical 𝜅 

is large. Regarding MACS, its clustering step is expected to deteriorate for high-dimensional corevectors; thus, one can expect better performance using avgpooling and kernel ’s compression if they successfully maintain the activations’ information.

## 6. Conclusions 

This paper presents a novel DR method with a controllable high-compression level for CLs’ activations to be used with OoD and AA detection methods. The method builds upon DMD’s and MACS’s DR methods; from the former, we leverage their intuition that LLFs are present across the layer’s output channels, and that simple average pooling is an effective way to achieve high-compression levels; from the latter, we leverage the trimmed SVD, which enables control over the compression level. To connect both DRs, and hence leverage their benefits, we propose using a CL isomorphism studied in Praggastis et al. (2022), which leads to relatively small dense matrices. We extend DMD’s and MACS’s implementations to enable them to accept generic DRs, and compare their performance with the different DR methods on OoD and AA detection. Results over standard OoD datasets and AAs show that the proposed method leads to detection AUCs comparable to or superior to their original ones, indicating that it captures the information contained within the layer’s activations, despite its inherently higher compression level. Furthermore, results show that the proposed method effectively reduces the memory and computation footprints of OoD/AA detection methods, contributing to the their broader applicability.
