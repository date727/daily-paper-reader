Abstract 

Curvature is often treated as an intrinsic property of a representation, although its empirical effect also depends on coordinate scale, learned logit temperature, and numerical safeguards. We study this interaction in hZACH-ViT, a compact Vision Transformer with Euclidean, Poincar´ e, and spherical prototype heads. The backbone architecture, seed-specific initialization, 50-per-class training subset, and optimization protocol are matched across three MedMNIST datasets and five seeds. At the fixed comparison curvature c = 1, Poincar´ e has the lowest class-macro PGD attack-success rate in all 12 dataset-budget cells and under a stronger CE+DLR multi-restart attack on all three datasets, but it also has the lowest clean MacroF1. An end-to-end curvature intervention changes the interpretation. Reducing Poincar´ e curvature to c = 0 .1 improves clean MacroF1 in every one of the 15 paired seed-dataset comparisons and removes hard boundary clipping, yet on OrganAMNIST it increases strong attack success from 89 .7% to 99 .3% (paired difference +9 .57 points; 95% hierarchical bootstrap CI [+5 .52 , +14 .03]). At c = 1, 40-47% of clean Poincar´ e features are hard-clipped, the radial Jacobian of the inherited map is nearly zero, and dimensionless attack trajectories are unusually long and inefficient. The spherical head provides a control: its curvature change is an exact scale gauge to floating-point precision and produces much smaller attack differences. These results do not establish intrinsic hyperbolic robustness. They identify an implementation-sensitive regime in which curvature, scale, and proximity to the Poincar´ e boundary jointly organize clean recognition and adversarial representation motion. 

Keywords: ZACH-ViT, hZACH-ViT, geometric deep learning, hyperbolic representations, adver-sarial robustness, curvature, low-data medical imaging 

# 1 Introduction 

Non-Euclidean representation learning replaces a usually implicit assumption - that useful features live in a flat vector space - with an explicit geometric design choice. Hyperbolic spaces provide exponentially expanding volume for hierarchical organization, while spherical representations em-phasize angular structure and remove radial scale (Nickel and Kiela, 2017; Ganea et al., 2018; Khrulkov et al., 2020; Mettes et al., 2019). These ideas have become established components of geometric deep learning and computer vision (Bronstein et al., 2017; Ermolov et al., 2022; Mettes et al., 2024). Yet a trained curved model is not determined by curvature sign alone. Curvature magnitude sets a coordinate radius; a learned temperature rescales metric logits; and practical Poincar´ e implementations clamp points and denominators near the boundary. Consequently, a 1arXiv:2610.00680v1 [cs.LG] 30 Sep 2026 nominally geometric observation can reflect an interaction between intrinsic geometry, representation adaptation, and finite-precision computation. This distinction is especially important under perturbation. Adversarial optimization provides a controlled way to interrogate which representation directions remain available and how a trajectory reaches a decision boundary. It is not, in our use, a claim about clinical security. Prior work has shown that robustness can depend on learned feature organization (Ilyas et al., 2019; Tsipras et al., 2019; Dapello et al., 2021), while recent work has begun to formulate attacks directly for hyperbolic networks (van Spengler et al., 2024). At the same time, Poincar´ e learning is known to face precision and optimization difficulties near its boundary (Sala et al., 2018; Mishne et al., 2023). High-dimensional hyperbolic prototype models can concentrate at a common near-boundary radius, sometimes erasing the practical distinction from fixed-radius Euclidean embeddings (Moreira et al., 2024); clipping has therefore been used deliberately to regulate hyperbolic feature norms (Guo et al., 2022). These observations motivate a more careful question than whether negative curvature is “robust”: which part of an apparent attack slowdown is preserved when curvature scale and boundary occupancy are changed? 

We answer this question in hZACH-ViT, the curved-latent extension of the compact, permutation-invariant ZACH-ViT backbone (Angelakis, 2026b,a). ZACH-ViT removes positional embeddings and the dedicated class token, uses global average pooling over patch representations, and contains approximately 0 .25M trainable parameters. hZACH-ViT retains this backbone and replaces the terminal classifier with geometry-aware prototypes. The earlier hZACH-ViT study treated geometry and curvature as dataset-dependent clean model-selection variables and found that low curvature was frequently preferred. The present work instead fixes the training and attack protocol, first comparing curvature signs at c = 1 and then intervening within the Poincar´ e and spherical families by retraining at c = 0 .1. The resulting contribution is fourfold. First, we provide a corrected class-macro attack comparison between linear, Euclidean-prototype, Poincar´ e, and spherical hZACH-ViT heads on identical shared-clean-correct samples. Second, we derive the curvature-scale gauge for both curved heads and use the spherical family as an exact numerical control. Third, we connect the Poincar´ e curvature intervention to normalized boundary occupancy, hard clipping, radial sensitivity, learned logit scale, and dimensionless attack trajectories. Fourth, we validate the central ordering with 20-step, three-restart CE and DLR attacks, per-sample worst-margin selection, convergence traces, and gradient diagnostics. The evidence supports an implementation-sensitive boundary regime, not a universal robustness property of negative curvature. 

# 2 Related Work and Positioning 

Prototypical networks classify by metric comparison with class representatives (Snell et al., 2017). Hyperbolic embeddings extend this construction to hierarchical structure (Nickel and Kiela, 2017, 2018; Ganea et al., 2018), including image embeddings and Vision Transformers (Khrulkov et al., 2020; Ermolov et al., 2022); hyperspherical classifiers instead remove feature magnitude and organize decisions through angles (Liu et al., 2017; Mettes et al., 2019). The practical meaning of curvature nevertheless depends on parameterization and precision (Sala et al., 2018; Mishne et al., 2023). Poincar´ e prototypes can concentrate with features at a common near-boundary radius (Moreira et al., 2024), while explicit radial clipping can change classification behavior (Guo et al., 2022). We therefore study curvature through normalized coordinates, hard projection, and radial sensitivity rather than treating its sign as a complete model description. Representation geometry can constrain accessible computation in biological and artificial sys-2tems (Sadtler et al., 2014; Chaudhuri et al., 2019; Gardner et al., 2022; Chung and Abbott, 2021). Under perturbation, feature organization determines which non-robust directions are available (Ilyas et al., 2019; Dapello et al., 2021), and direct attacks on hyperbolic networks show that evaluation itself can depend on geometry (van Spengler et al., 2024). A CVPR 2026 PHAROS workshop study evaluated common corruptions and first-order adversarial attacks for the flat ZACH-ViT backbone (Angelakis and Gomez-Barrero, 2026), while the clean hZACH-ViT study examined geometry-aware heads as model-selection alternatives (Angelakis, 2026a). Neither work separated curvature sign from curvature scale, audited the curvature-scale gauge, or analyzed adversarial motion in dimensionless curved coordinates. Here attacks serve as representation probes, not evidence of deployment security.

# 4 Experimental Protocol 

Datasets and low-data training. We use BloodMNIST (8 classes), DermaMNIST (7), and OrganAMNIST (11) from MedMNIST v2 (Yang et al., 2023). This family-specific contrast argues against explaining the result by the nominal manifold radius alone. 65.3 Boundary occupancy, radial sensitivity, and the curvature gauge 

At c = 1, mean seed-level median Poincar´ e radius rounds to 1 .000 on every dataset, and 40 .1-47 .2% of clean features reach the hard projection threshold (Table 2). At c = 0 .1, clipping disappears in all 15 runs and representations move inward. Mean median radial Jacobians increase from 1 .0 × 10 −5,1.8 × 10 −5, and 3 .3 × 10 −5 to 3 .7 × 10 −2, 5 .6 × 10 −2, and 1 .11 × 10 −1 on Blood, Derma, and Organ, respectively. The intervention therefore changes access to radial representation motion as well as clean fit. Learned temperature does not compensate: Poincar´ e’s mean active scale is 1 .139 at both curvatures, so s/ √c changes from 1 .139 to 3 .603. In a functional gauge audit, spherical rescaling changes logits by at most 1 .43 × 10 −6 across 133,242 logits and never changes a prediction. The boundary-saturated Poincar´ e 1 → 0.1 transform instead reaches a maximum difference of 0 .713 and changes one prediction among 62,572 logits; the reverse transform changes none. Thus Eq. (4) is exact in ideal arithmetic but not at the inherited Poincar´ e boundary. Full directional errors appear in Appendix B. 

5.4 Dimensionless trajectories and attack validity 

Normalizing metric radius does not remove the trajectory signature. At 4 /255, Poincar´ e c = 1 efficiencies are 0 .120, 0 .123, and 0 .118 on Blood, Derma, and Organ, versus 0 .155, 0 .207, and 0 .211 at c = 0 .1. Dimensionless c = 1 paths are three to five times longer, and 40 .4-57 .9% of their states are clipped; no c = 0 .1 state is clipped. Spherical changes are smaller and not directionally uniform (Fig. 2d; Appendix E). All 60 curvature-comparison combinations have finite, non-zero clean input gradients. On OrganAMNIST, CE and DLR strengthen through 20 steps but retain the same curvature separation; DLR also contributes selected worst-margin candidates. These checks narrow the interpretation from generic gradient failure to the measured boundary regime, without making PGD a certificate (Appendix C). A frozen-backbone control further shows that a Poincar´ e terminal distance alone does not reproduce the end-to-end ordering (Appendix D); representation adaptation is part of the effect. 

5.5 Frozen-backbone attribution control 

To separate terminal distance from learned representation adaptation, we freeze each selected linear BloodMNIST backbone and train only Euclidean, Poincar´ e, and spherical prototype heads. On the strict direct four-head pool, five-seed PGD-10 ASR at 4 /255 is 98 .0% (linear), 95 .0% (Euclidean prototype), 97 .5% (Poincar´ e), and 96 .9% (sphere). Poincar´ e is therefore not the slowest head on this common representation. Because several seed pools contain only 3-40 attacked images, this is an attribution control rather than a robustness ranking. Together with the end-to-end intervention, it rejects the strongest head-only interpretation: the c = 1 ordering depends on interaction among the training objective, the adapted backbone, and the boundary-sensitive map (Appendix D). 

# 6 Discussion 

The c = 1 result admits a tempting narrative - Poincar´ e representations fail more slowly - but the intervention makes it incomplete. Lower curvature improves every paired Poincar´ e clean result, removes saturation, restores radial sensitivity, and removes the large Organ attack gap. The same change is an almost exact numerical gauge for the sphere and has a much smaller adversarial effect. This connects near-boundary concentration in prototype learning (Moreira et al., 2024), 7precision-dependent hyperbolic optimization (Sala et al., 2018; Mishne et al., 2023), and the effects of radial clipping (Guo et al., 2022): boundary occupancy here coincides with extinguished radial sensitivity and inefficient normalized attack paths. The symmetry distinction is precise. Spherical normalization quotients every positive radial orbit, so the feature-loss gradient is tangent and attacks act by rotating direction. Poincar´ e geometry retains radius intrinsically, yet the implemented c = 1 map often suppresses radial sensitivity at the numerical boundary. One is an exact architectural invariance; the other is a curvature-dependent finite-precision regime. Because c = 1 and c = 0 .1 models are trained independently, the study establishes their coordinated changes but does not prove that clipping alone causes the ASR gap. 

# 7 Limitations and Broader Impact 

We study one compact architecture, three medical benchmarks, five seeds, and 50 examples per class. Results may change with full-data training, larger backbones, other Poincar´ e maps, or adversarial training. Shared-clean-correct evaluation excludes classes without jointly correct images, and the stabilized trajectory calculation need not duplicate the classifier’s clamp sequence bit-for-bit. The attacks are strong first-order tests, not exhaustive or certified evaluations; ASR is high for every head at 4 /255. No configuration is clinically secure. The diagnostic lesson is that curvature and boundary numerics must be audited together before attributing failure to intrinsic geometry. 

# 8 Conclusion 

At c = 1, Poincar´ e hZACH-ViT fails more slowly in every core cell, but pays a clean-recognition cost. At c = 0 .1, that cost, hard clipping, and the large Organ attack slowdown disappear, whereas the spherical gauge control changes much less. The supported claim is therefore narrower than “hyperbolic geometry is robust”: curvature can place a learned Poincar´ e representation in a boundary-saturated numerical regime that jointly reorganizes clean recognition and adversarial motion .

# A Evaluation-pool coverage 

The four-head c = 1 core pools contain 454-512 images per BloodMNIST seed, 176-205 per DermaMNIST seed, and 423-704 per OrganAMNIST seed after the independent class cap. All BloodMNIST pools contain all eight classes. DermaMNIST consistently lacks class 3 in the strict four-head intersection. OrganAMNIST covers 8-11 classes depending on seed. The strong pools use an independent cap of 16 images per eligible class. The within-family curvature pools are larger because they require agreement only between c = 1 and c = 0 .1. Poincar´ e core pools contain 502-512 BloodMNIST images, 207-275 DermaMNIST images, and 554-703 OrganAMNIST images; their strong counterparts contain 128, 89-103, and 144-176 images, respectively. The spherical curvature pools contain all 11 OrganAMNIST classes in every seed. Exact per-class counts and selected indices are included in the supplementary package.

# C Strong-attack convergence and gradients 

Across the curvature comparison, the maximum observed zero-gradient fraction and non-finite-gradient fraction are both zero. Median clean input-gradient L2 norms are larger at c = 0 .1: for Poincar´ e they average 0 .272 versus 0 .234 on Blood, 0 .551 versus 0 .254 on Derma, and 3 .72 versus 2.55 on OrganAMNIST. DLR supplies the worst-margin candidate for a non-zero fraction of images in every dataset family, so the combined result is not a relabeling of CE-only PGD.
