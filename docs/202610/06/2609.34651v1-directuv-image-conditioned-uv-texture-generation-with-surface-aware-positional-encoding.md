## 摘要
为3D网格生成高质量的UV纹理仍然具有挑战性。多视角投影管道存在遮挡和视角不一致的问题，而近期直接在UV空间生成纹理的方法仍依赖辅助模块提供3D信息，导致注意力机制与UV网格位置绑定而非底层表面几何。这种不匹配限制了接缝和断开UV岛之间的连贯性。我们提出DirectUV，一种基于图像条件的UV纹理扩散框架，在预训练图像VAE的潜在UV空间中运行，该框架根据单张输入图像和粗略UV图对UV潜在变量进行去噪。其核心是Surface-Aware Positional Encoding（SAPE），它用通过UV到表面对应关系获得的每个标记的3D表面坐标替代标准的2D网格位置编码。由于位置编码定义了注意力使用的距离度量，SAPE使标记能够根据表面对应的位置接近度进行交互，而非UV网格距离，从而恢复接缝和断开岛之间的连贯性。多级扩展进一步将不同的注意力头分配给同一潜在UV块的逐步更细的子区域，使模型能够以多种粒度推理表面结构。实验表明，DirectUV在遮挡区域和未见过视角区域生成更清晰且全局一致的纹理，相较于其他基线方法有显著改进。

## Abstract
Generating high-quality UV textures for 3D meshes remains challenging. Multi-view projection pipelines suffer from occlusion and view inconsistency, and recent methods that generate textures directly in UV space still rely on auxiliary modules to supply 3D information, leaving the attention mechanism tied to UV-grid positions rather than to the underlying surface geometry. This mismatch limits coherence across seams and disconnected UV islands.   We propose DirectUV, an image-conditioned UV texture diffusion framework that operates in the latent UV space of a pretrained image VAE, in which a Diffusion Transformer denoises the UV latent given a single input image and a coarse UV map. At its core, Surface-Aware Positional Encoding (SAPE) replaces the standard 2D-grid positional encoding with encodings derived from per-token 3D surface coordinates obtained via UV-to-surface correspondence. As positional encodings define the distance metric used by attention, SAPE enables tokens to interact according to 3D positional proximity derived from surface correspondence rather than UV-grid distance, restoring coherence across seams and disconnected islands. A multi-level extension further assigns different attention heads to progressively finer subdivisions of the same latent UV patch, allowing the model to reason about surface structure at multiple granularities.   Experiments show that DirectUV produces sharper and more globally consistent textures than other baselines, with the largest improvements in occluded and view-unseen regions where projection-based methods leave gaps or stretched textures.
