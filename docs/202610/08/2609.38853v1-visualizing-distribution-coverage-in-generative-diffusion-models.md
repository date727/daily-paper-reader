## 摘要
扩散蒸馏被广泛用于加速采样，且由此产生的少步模型普遍被认为能够匹配甚至超越其多步教师模型的生成能力。然而，标准评估方法如GenEval2通常仅对每个提示生成一个样本，因此改进的分数可能无法揭示分布覆盖的损失。因此，我们重新审视了少步模型是否在单次抽样表现之外真正匹配其教师模型，使用\textbf{pass@$\mathbf{k}$}这一指标来衡量$k$次独立抽样中至少有一次满足质量标准的概率。当$k{=}1$时，pass@$k$退化为标准的单次抽样评估。随着$k$增大，曲线揭示了额外抽样是否能找到真正不同的成功案例，还是仅仅重复相同的模式，从而直接暴露模型覆盖有效输出空间的广度。我们首先表明，其质量-覆盖权衡已被广泛研究的无分类器引导（CFG）是最清晰的案例：更高的引导改善pass@$1$，但其优势在更大的$k$时会缩小并反转。将pass@$k$应用于少步蒸馏模型时，我们发现相同的权衡沿训练目标分裂：分布匹配目标会集中学生的输出分布，提升早期命中率但削弱大预算覆盖能力；而一致性与轨迹目标则更好地保留教师的覆盖能力，即使在较大的$k$时也是如此。我们进一步表明这种权衡也适用于少步因果视频生成。我们的发现揭示了扩散蒸馏之前被忽视的成本：在图像和视频生成中，训练目标的选择从根本上决定了少步模型是否继承其教师的分布覆盖能力，或是以单次抽样质量为代价进行交换。

## Abstract
Diffusion distillation is widely adopted to accelerate sampling, and the resulting few-step models are broadly believed to match or even surpass their multi-step teachers in generation. However, standard evaluations such as GenEval2 typically draw only one sample per prompt, so improved scores may fail to reveal losses in distribution coverage. We therefore revisit whether distilled models truly match their teachers beyond single-draw performance using \textbf{pass@$\mathbf{k}$}, which measures the probability that at least one of $k$ independent samples satisfies a quality criterion. At $k{=}1$, pass@$k$ reduces to standard single-draw evaluation. As $k$ grows, the curve reveals whether additional draws find genuinely different successes or merely revisit the same modes, directly exposing how broadly a model covers the space of valid outputs. We first show that classifier-free guidance (CFG), whose quality--coverage tradeoff is well established, is the clearest case: higher guidance improves pass@$1$, but its advantage shrinks and reverses at larger $k$. Applying pass@$k$ to few-step distilled models, we find the same tradeoff splits along training objectives: distribution-matching objectives concentrate the student's output distribution, boosting early-hit rates while eroding large-budget coverage, whereas consistency and trajectory-based objectives better preserve the teacher's coverage even at large $k$. We further show that this tradeoff extends to few-step causal video generation. Our findings reveal a previously overlooked cost of diffusion distillation: across both image and video generation, the choice of training objective fundamentally determines whether a few-step model inherits its teacher's distribution coverage or trades it away for single-draw quality.
