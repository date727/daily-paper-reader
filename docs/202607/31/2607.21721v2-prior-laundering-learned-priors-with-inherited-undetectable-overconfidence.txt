# Prior laundering: learned priors with inherited, undetectable overconfidence 

Ali Siahkoohi alisk@ucf.edu Department of Computer Science University of Central Florida 

Sina Alemohammad sina.alemohammad@austin.utexas.edu Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering The University of Texas at Austin 

# Abstract 

Learned generative priors now supply the regularization in ill-posed imaging inverse problems, and the uncertainty read from their posterior samples is taken as evidence earned from data. When examples of the true image are scarce, as in seismic and medical imaging, the widely adopted recourse is to train such a prior not on truths but on an archive of past reconstructions— prior laundering . We show that the uncertainty it then reports can be overconfident, and that no measurement-side check can reveal it. On the directions a forward operator leaves unresolved, this prior reports not what the data support but the assumption built into the older reconstruction method. More specifically, when the archive holds posterior samples, its population law—averaged over the measurements—is exactly the old regularizer advanced a single expectation–maximization step, frozen on the operator’s blind subspace. The freeze leaves no signature in the data. Two truths differing only there induce identical data laws, so no goodness-of-fit test separates them, and self-consistency diagnostics, simulation-based calibration among them, pass whatever the prior believes. In the more realistic case, where the archive keeps a single-best reconstruction rather than posterior samples, the blind credible interval collapses to zero width. We prove these statements and demonstrate the inherited overconfidence on deployed seismic and groundwater imaging against a truth-trained control. We recommend reporting which directions the operator resolves—separating the confidence the data support from belief inherited through the pipeline. 

# 1 Introduction 

Due to their nonuniqueness, ill-posed inverse problems call for a prior. A learned prior—a deep generative model of plausible solutions—is increasingly used in place of a handcrafted one, and the posterior it induces yields sharper reconstructions (Jalal et al., 2021; Chung et al., 2023) with uncertainty read off posterior samples (Siahkoohi et al., 2022). Training one, however, requires examples of the truth, which seismic and medical imaging can rarely supply. In practice, the common recourse is to train the prior on an archive of legacy reconstructions —i.e., the outputs of an older method (Jalal et al., 2021)—and then treat it as data-driven, its reported uncertainty included. In seismic imaging, generative priors are trained on archives of migrated or inverted sections and on legacy velocity models (Siahkoohi et al., 2021; 2022; 2023; Baldassari et al., 2023); in medical imaging, priors for accelerated MRI, the standard low-dose CT benchmarks, and transcranial-ultrasound models are alike built on reconstructed scans rather than any pristine truth (Jalal et al., 2021; Leuschner et al., 2021; Naftchi-Ardebili et al., 2026)—a reuse of already-processed data the imaging community has warned against as implicit data crimes (Shimron et al., 2022). 1arXiv:2607.21721v2 [stat.ML] 30 Jul 2026 0

In this work we characterize what prior laundering leads to—what an archive-trained prior believes, where that belief comes from, and which part of it no measurement can correct. Averaged over the measurements actually collected, the archive’s population law is the old regularizer pushed one expectation–maximization (EM) step toward the truth—the true prior whose samples the setting lacks. This step moves the prior toward the truth on the directions the operator resolves—in the marginal-likelihood sense the identity makes precise—and leaves those it never sees frozen at the old assumption . The learned prior carries that frozen belief into deployment. We call this prior laundering : a handcrafted assumption, passed through a reconstruction pipeline, re-emerges wearing the credibility of data—not deception but a structural consequence of the pipeline (Figure 1.1). What lifts this above the textbook fact that a prior fills the directions the data leave undetermined is that the overconfidence is undetectable . On a blind direction the reported uncertainty is the inherited assumption’s, scored against the spread the truth actually has; when that assumption is tighter than the truth, the credible interval is overconfident by an amount we give in closed form in the linear–Gaussian case. The error has a direction—a looser prior would over-cover, which is merely conservative—and the tight case is the one the practice selects: the penalties that make a reconstruction look clean, smoothness and sparsity, suppress variation indiscriminately, the undetermined directions included, and the classical asymptotics record the same signed failure (Knapik et al., 2011). Because the shortfall lives entirely on the subspace the operator never probes, nothing a practitioner can compute reveals it, and the reason divides in two. A check run against the recorded data has no power because the measurements carry no information about those directions at all: two truths that differ only on the blind subspace share the data law (Theorem 4.4). A self-consistency check is uninformative for a different reason: simulation-based calibration (Talts et al., 2018; Hermans et al., 2022), the field standard, is neutral by construction, its ranks uniform whatever the prior believes. These two families cover the checks deployment supplies; what lies outside them is information from beyond the survey—ground truth on the blind subspace, or the added channel of Theorem 4.7 (Theorem 4.5). 2Unfortunately, the most common form of the practice is worse still. Real archives store a single-best (maximum-a-posteriori) reconstruction rather than a posterior sample: one migrated section per survey, one reconstructed image per patient (Veritas, 2005; Leuschner et al., 2021). Single-best curation collapses the blind-direction report to zero width—certainty where the truth genuinely varies—for any forward operator with a blind subspace. It asks nothing else of the pipeline: any noise reaching the unknown only through the operator, any variational penalty, improper ones included; what collapses is the conditional credible interval on a blind functional (Theorem 4.6). Whether this idealized overconfidence survives deployment depends on the generative model’s capacity: a prior too weak to fit the archive would mask the inherited belief with spread of its own (Koehler et al., 2021). Priors expressive enough to fit it—a diffusion model (Ho et al., 2020) and a deep hierarchical coupling flow (Kruse et al., 2021)—recover the pattern the theory predicts on both deployed operators (Section 5). Section 2 places the work among its neighbors; Sections 3 and 4 set up the problem and establish the theory; Section 5 asks whether the idealized effect survives deployment, on two operators and in two prior families; Section 6 sets out the remedies and their limits. 

# 2 Related work 

Priors trained on the wrong data The closest precursors train a prior on data that stand in for the truth and find it carries the substitute’s errors: Barco et al. (2025) correct such a prior by retraining against real measurements, and Siahkoohi and Sabeddu (2026) document learned geophysical priors memorizing their archives. There the defect belongs to the training set and retraining reaches it; our point is the part it cannot—on the operator’s blind subspace the measurements say nothing, so the inherited belief sits beyond correction of that kind (Theorem A.6). 

The resolved and blind subspaces The split into directions the data inform and directions they leave to the prior is standard, under a different name in each field: the likelihood-informed subspace of Cui et al. and Spantini et al. (2014; 2015), the active subspace of Constantine et al. (2014), and, in geophysics, the resolution analysis of Backus and Gilbert (1968), whose model-resolution operator names which combinations the data determine. That line uses the split to compress, treating the blind complement as a benign truncation; we put it to the opposite use and audit what the prior reports there. 

Identifiability, and the empirical-Bayes iteration The sensing theorems of Tachella, Chen, and Davies (2023) settle when the signal distribution is learnable from incomplete measurements: beyond the range space, only under operator diversity or a group invariance tying the blind directions to the resolved. Rozet et al. (2024) learn a diffusion prior by exactly that map—one step of the classical nonparametric-maximum-likelihood iteration for a mixing density (Vardi et al., 1985), the empirical-Bayes program of Robbins (1956) in its modern, “learn the prior” form—run to a fixed point. A legacy archive is the same iteration stopped after one step: we work on its complement, one fixed operator with no invariance assumed, and ask what that step reports on the fiber no step ever moves (Theorem 4.2), where the blind conditional cannot be learned (Theorem 4.4). Operator diversity returns as the design statement of Theorem 4.7. 

Coverage of credible sets, and non-identifiability Whether a Bayesian credible set has frequentist coverage is the subject of the Bernstein–von Mises theory for inverse problems (Knapik et al., 2011; Szabó et al., 2015), which establishes coverage along the directions the data eventually resolve. Our result is the structural complement: along a genuine blind subspace the shortfall is constant in the sample size and not a function of the data marginal, and its undetectability is the non-identifiability of the blind parameters under a nontrivial kernel (Theorem 4.4)—a regime injectivity precludes. The same audit—whether the truth stays inside the reported interval—was recently posed for finite-element discretization error by Poot et al. (2026) and, for learned imaging priors, by Thong et al. (2024), who find by Monte Carlo replication that diffusion and plug-and-play posteriors generally misreport their probabilities; we supply the mechanism and prove the shortfall undetectable on the operator’s blind subspace. 3Point estimates, recursive collapse, and miscalibrated learned posteriors What a single-best reconstruction is worth as a posterior summary, and what spread it discards, is a classical debate (Gribonval, 2011; Burger & Lucka, 2014; Bassett & Deride, 2019); we isolate the operator-level sharpening: on the blind subspace the discard is total. Training a model on its own outputs collapses it over many recursive rounds (Alemohammad et al., 2024; Shumailov et al., 2024); ours is a single step against real measurements, and the hazard is what that step leaves frozen rather than a drift to degeneracy. Learned posteriors (Adler & Öktem, 2018) are miscalibrated in ways calibration tests can catch (Hermans et al., 2022); ours is the undetectable dual.

# 4 Training on legacy reconstructions is one step from the old assumption 

Averaged over the real measurements, a whole archive of legacy reconstructions is the handcrafted regularizer advanced one EM step toward the truth: improved where the data resolve, frozen where they cannot (§4.1); the frozen belief carries a closed-form coverage shortfall no measurement-side check can detect (§4.2); single-best curation collapses the blind credible interval to zero width (§4.3). Remark 4.5 (Self-consistency checks are neutral by construction) . Simulation-based calibration draws a test truth from the model’s own prior, simulates a measurement from it, samples the posterior and ranks the truth among the draws, so it reads nothing from the recorded data and Theorem 4.4 does not reach it. A separate argument does: whenever the sampler targets the prior that generated the test truth, that truth and the posterior draws are exchangeable, so the rank of any one-dimensional functional is uniform whatever the prior (Talts et al., 2018). This check scores a prior’s consistency with its own posterior, never its fidelity to the truth: its law is the same for the curated prior as for a truth-trained one, its power against the freeze equal to its size. The oracle prior is trained on broadband reflectivity built above the archive’s band from the survey’s own tracked horizons (Wu & Fomel, 2018), the curated prior on the least-squares-migration archive; Section B gives the acquisition, reconstruction, and training details.

5.3 The prior-laundering setup 

On each deployed operator we train deep generative priors: one curated from the legacy reconstructions and, as a control, one trained on the true images (oracle); architectures and budgets are matched within each pair (Section B). A prior too weak to fit its target would add its own spread on the blind subspace—the same whether it is trained on the archive or on the truth, an over-dispersion of the model’s, not the archive’s, consistent with the spectral bias of neural networks (Rahaman et al., 2019)—and mask the inherited belief; the protocol therefore gates on capacity. The expressive diffusion and flow priors used below do fit their targets—validation losses fall to a plateau—so their blind reports reflect the archive rather than under-training. Because the blind posterior conditional is the prior’s (Section 3), the blind report is read from the prior’s marginal, the protocol-matched choice of Section 5.2. This blind read is a property of the prior rather than of the sampler: the likelihood residual cannot reach the null, AN = 0, and where a per-pixel normalization would re-route it—the seismic illumination basis—the applied update’s scored-blind component is removed at every step and the residual action is measured. 

5.4 Seismic Born imaging 

On the seismic operator of Section 5.1, blind coverage falls short of nominal at the standard levels, beyond the bootstrap intervals, for the curated prior, whose blind spread lies on the migration archive’s, far tighter than the truth’s, while the oracle errs wide—the conservative footing of the source-calibration κ—and does not under-cover; the resolved control passes—conservatively at low levels, where both priors over-cover, the curated further (Figure 5.1, top). The deployment also makes the defect physical. Sampling one survey’s posterior under each prior—diffusion posterior sampling (Chung et al., 2023) on the Born operator—returns reconstructions that share the same reflectors, both fitting the recorded data (Figure 1.1); their uncertainty parts along the operator’s illumination, the curated prior’s spread narrowest exactly where the illumination is weakest—and ranked by illumination the two spreads’ gradients run opposite (Figure 1.1e,f), a property of the section rather than of a few pixels. The closed-form law of Theorem 4.3 is not the yardstick here—its linear–Gaussian hypotheses do not hold for a migration archive and a diffusion prior—so the theory supplies the direction of the effect and the freeze that causes it, not its magnitude (Theorem A.4).

(2024), whose population idealization is the loop of Theorem A.6—heals the resolved subspace and leaves the blind conditional exactly where it was. The moves that stay inside the survey reach less: archiving posterior samples rather than a single-best reconstruction spares the blind report the zero-width collapse of Theorem 4.6—the regularizer’s own spread, overconfident where tight but finite—while pooling several methods reports their disagreement, not the truth. Scoring coverage requires a known truth, and that requirement bounds the realism of the image class rather than the existence of a reference. Our deployed truths are necessarily constructed, for the reason the seismic dataset makes concrete—a migrated section is band-limited, so the broadband truths are built above the archive’s band: any field modality whose only reference is a legacy reconstruction faces the same circularity. Since the blind subspace is fixed by the operator and not by the distribution the truth is drawn from, the image class sets the size and sign of the shortfall, never where it strikes. The directions an inverse problem leaves blind are exactly the directions where a learned prior’s reported uncertainty cannot be checked against the measurements, and a prior trained on legacy reconstructions reports the old regularizer’s belief there with the credibility of data. The operator names those directions in advance of any prior, so the separation costs nothing beyond what the forward model already supplies.

# B Experimental details 

This appendix records the acquisition, legacy reconstruction, and generative-prior training for the two deployed operators of Section 5; every run regenerates from the released code, which fixes the random seeds and pins the environment. Within each oracle/curated pair the two priors are matched in architecture, data budget, optimizer, and random-number state and reset before each arm, so they differ only in the training target (Section 5.3); Table B.1 collects the settings. 

Seismic: acquisition and legacy reconstruction. On the linearized-Born operator of Section 5.1 (geometry there), wavelet-colored noise is added to every shot. The legacy archive is a sparsity-promoting least-squares migration: from the source-averaged reverse-time migration we minimize the shot-data misfit under an ℓ1 penalty on the reflectivity through five epochs of stochastic per-shot proximal gradient—one shot per step, AdaGrad-preconditioned to tame the illumination imbalance—with the soft threshold fixed from the plain migration’s amplitude and the accumulated step size (value in the code). The curated prior trains on this least-squares-migration archive, the oracle on broadband reflectivity built above the archive’s band from the survey’s tracked horizons (Wu & Fomel, 2018). 

Seismic: diffusion priors. Each of the two priors is a diffusers UNet2DModel (64 base channels, attention at one scale) with a cosine-β schedule over 1000 steps (Ho et al., 2020), trained by AdamW under a one-cycle schedule (peak learning rate 2 × 10 −4, batch size 8, 3000 steps, gradient-norm clipping) on per-pixel-standardized images and sampled with the ancestral update, clip_sample disabled (a default [−1, 1] 

clamp collapses standardized samples). That both priors fit their targets—their unconditional samples of a kind with the training images (Figure B.1), their validation denoising loss fallen to a plateau (Figure B.2)—is what charges the deployed overconfidence to the training target rather than to under-training (Section 5.3). 

Groundwater: flow priors. Each of the two priors is a HINT normalizing flow (Kruse et al., 2021)—a binary-tree affine-coupling flow (four coupling blocks, tree depth two, three-layer coupling networks) over the d = 100 Karhunen–Loève coefficients—trained by maximum likelihood with per-feature standardization, Adam under a polynomial-decay schedule, a small dequantization noise that removes the low-dimensional over-concentration the collapsed archive would otherwise drive the flow into, and validation-based early stopping.
